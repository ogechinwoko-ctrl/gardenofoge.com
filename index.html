Our manifesto

Like a garden, community doesn't happen automatically. It has to be planted. It has to be nurtured. It has to be protected.

Why we exist

People are more connected digitally than ever, and more disconnected socially. When community is properly cultivated, it produces something beautiful.

“People grow better together.”

Garden of Oge creates intentional spaces for people to reconnect: with themselves, with each other, with creativity, with culture, and with nature. It's a garden, wellness studio, coworking space, cultural hub, and neighborhood gathering place, built for all people and especially for the diaspora.

Our mission

To create a welcoming space that uplifts people and community through art, creativity, wellness, education, food, nature, and meaningful connection.

The Garden experience
  1. You arrive.
  2. There is greenery everywhere.
  3. Someone is creating art.
  4. People are talking.
  5. Food is being prepared.
  6. A DJ is playing something soulful.
  7. You don't feel rushed. You feel welcomed.

“You found your village.”

Community care projects

What we're building

Beyond the programs, two longer-term projects: a food-is-medicine wellness campus, and a policy practice that carries what we learn into the rules that shape community health.

Our guiding light

The community quarterback model developed by Purpose Built Communities, where one local organization leads a neighborhood's revitalization, coordinating partners across housing, education, and health so the work stays rooted in the people who live there.

In progress · Project site 01

Mechanicsville Community Wellness Campus

Healthier communities through thoughtful design, sustainable practice, and collaboration.

A food-is-medicine wellness campus in one of Atlanta's historic but underinvested neighborhoods. It brings together a community teaching garden, culturally anchored wellness programming, and short-term transitional housing pathways.

Program objectives
  1. Increase access to short-term housing and fresh produce in Mechanicsville
  2. Provide health screenings and food interventions for residents
  3. Deliver culturally relevant community programming and art therapy
  4. Build a sustainable community food–housing–health ecosystem

Interdisciplinary policy reform practice

Archives & Advocacy

Turning what we learn on the ground into better policy.

Our projects generate real-world data about what actually helps a neighborhood get well. Archives & Advocacy elevates that evidence and puts it to work, examining the policies that shape community health and pushing for reform where it matters most. Our monthly People's Table town halls are where this work meets the neighborhood.

Where we look
  1. Elevate real-world data generated by our community care projects
  2. Examine local policy at the city, county, and state level
  3. Examine healthcare and social services policy more broadly
  4. Advocate for reform at the points with the most leverage

Who we are

Rooted in Atlanta

We reimagine urban land in Atlanta as a source of nourishment, turning underused lots into multi-use green spaces for gardens, outdoor wellness, and place-based programming.

Ogechi Nwoko, MD, MBAFounder & Executive Director

A physician innovator who believes community health starts with the land a neighborhood stands on and the food it can reach. Ogechi founded Garden of Oge to put that belief into the ground.

More about Ogechi →
Coletrane WilliamsLand Director

A cultural worker and multidisciplinary artist, Coletrane leads the land itself: the soil, the beds, the structures, and the growing seasons that turn our sites into working gardens for food and a stage for community cultural expression. From the first build in Mechanicsville to every harvest after, Coletrane keeps the campus growing.

Daryl Singleton, MDProgram Director

A family medicine physician who grew up in metro Atlanta, with Mississippi roots, Daryl brings a relationship-focused, holistic approach to the programs he shapes, from Grow Nites to Sunday Tune Ups. He lives by a simple mantra: “When you care for your body, your body will care for you.” Off the clock, find him in nature, at a concert, or with a guitar.
